Law school, legal aid, or law firm? Use the practicum on your own cases

The Legal Practicum System is an innovative teaching and learning tool for law students and lawyers. The Practicum System is designed for lawyers at any level.

Try an experiment. Take a risk. Receive immediate feedback. Try again. Any time. No waiting in line. No coffee breaks. 24/7. A classroom of one. It is yours whenever you want.

The law practice and law practice management skills

Law practice skills

  • Ability to diagnose and plan solutions for legal problems
  • Ability in legal analysis and reasoning
  • Knowledge of substantive law
  • Knowledge of procedural law
  • Library legal research
  • Computer legal research
  • Fact gathering
  • Oral communication
  • Written communication
  • Counseling
  • Instilling others' confidence in you
  • Ability to obtain and keep clients
  • Negotiation
  • Litigation
  • Organization and management of legal work
  • Sensitivity to professional and ethical concerns
  • Drafting legal documents
  • Prompt formulation and iteration
  • AI-output verification and citation checking
  • Judgment in technology-supported work
  • Confidentiality and privilege protection in AI-assisted work

Law practice management skills

  • Fee arrangements, pricing, billing
  • Human resources, hiring, support staff
  • Capitalization, investment
  • Project and time management, efficiency
  • Planning, resource allocation, budgeting
  • Marketing, client development
  • Technology, computers, communications
  • Governance, decision-making, long-range strategic planning
  • Interpersonal communications, staff relations
  • Data and matter hygiene with AI tools

For more than 50 years, a group of exceptional people — law students, law teachers, lawyers from firms large and small, judges, and non-lawyers — have worked together to develop the Legal Practicum System to assist law students and lawyers to learn, build, enhance, experiment, test ideas, strategy, and techniques, and take risks without fear of negative consequences.

Emerging technologies will help lawyers at all levels provide legal services quickly and at appropriate cost. Justice for all.

The Legal Practicum System is an open resource tool for all lawyers and law students at no cost. It may be edited and used to develop, learn, enhance, and perfect legal practice and legal management skills.

The Legal Practicum System is available to law students and lawyers throughout their careers, at any time, and is adaptable to any jurisdiction.

ABOUT THE METHOD

People first; technology as the tool

Every exercise here ships with a machine-readable rubric and a revise-and-repeat loop. Faculty coach students through live work with people. Technology supplies more practice repetitions under faculty supervision. Technology gives the first-pass assessment and feedback, at any hour. The simulated client interviews, rubric feedback, and firm dashboard below support that practice.

WHO WROTE IT

Authors

John Sonsteng

Author

John Sonsteng is an emeritus professor at Mitchell Hamline School of Law. He was a trial lawyer before he became a teacher. John has written many books and articles about advocacy. He is a Fulbright scholar and has taught effective advocacy skills throughout the United States and in many other countries.

Damien Riehl

Co-author & Provocateur

Damien Riehl is a lawyer-technologist with deep experience in litigation, cybersecurity, and AI. Admitted in 2002 and coding since 1985, he clerked for chief judges, litigated complex cases, and led global digital forensics teams. He now builds AI-driven legal systems. He co-chairs Minnesota's Connected and Automated Vehicles Council and chairs the MSBA AI Committee, advancing access-to-justice initiatives. He helped develop SALI's 18,000+ legal data standards and contributes to FOLIO's legal ontology. At Clio, he integrates AI into large-scale legal datasets. He also engages AI ethics work with global Catholic leaders and created the "All the Music" public-domain project.

Abby Breit, J.D.

Editor

Abby Breit edits the Legal Practicum.
