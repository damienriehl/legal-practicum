## Purpose {#b:ee77ae52}

Use technology to help you run a law practice, then check and revise its output yourself. Work from the [firm dashboard](../firm/index.html) for Ellingboe & Ravndal. Use a tool your school selects. You remain responsible for the numbers, advice, public claims, and handling of information; the tool does not make professional decisions for you. {#b:c1e0bfd3}

### Your objectives {#b:f2af2ede}

- The student will demonstrate financial judgment by checking every number in a technology-assisted management memo against the dashboard and explaining priorities to the partners. {#b:e95c2f35}
- The student will demonstrate responsible client development by revising a practice-area marketing plan and public blurb against Model Rules 7.1–7.3. {#b:594e29a7}
- The student will demonstrate competence, confidentiality, and supervision by writing a firm technology policy grounded in Model Rules 1.1, 1.6, and 5.3 and ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024). {#b:55d160d2}

This exercise develops SK-PM-10 (Data and matter hygiene with AI tools), budgeting tasks TSK-080 and TSK-082, and marketing and client-development tasks TSK-083, TSK-084, and TSK-085. {#b:9054ee27}

## The template {#b:33081374}

### 1. Prepare safe inputs {#b:1374fadd}

Never paste client-identifying information into a tool. The matters are fictional, but practice the same confidentiality habits you would use with an actual file. Use aggregate dashboard figures and anonymous descriptions of practice areas. Keep names, contact details, matter captions, identifiers, and identifying factual details out of your prompts. Record the tool you used and save your sanitized prompts, first drafts, corrections, and final versions for your instructor. {#b:d6ff6477}

### 2. Monthly management memo {#b:9575acfe}

Ask the tool to draft a one-page monthly management memo to the partners using realization, collection, AR over 90 days, trust balance, fee revenue against plan, and the book of business. State the dashboard's as-of date and reporting period. The dashboard is a single trailing-12-month snapshot: use it for this month's management discussion without presenting its figures as one month's results or inventing changes from earlier months. {#b:e8283fb5}

Check every number yourself against the dashboard, including any percentages or calculations the tool adds. Keep a verification note listing each figure, its dashboard source, and any correction. Distinguish worked value, billed fees, and collections when discussing the book of business. Explain how realization and collection affect available cash, what overdue receivables need attention, and how fee revenue compares with plan. Keep client trust money separate from funds available to operate the firm. {#b:4d9d0bb5}

Recommend two management actions, name who should carry each out, and state when the partners should review progress. For TSK-080, use the published fee-revenue budget as the basis for a proposed budget decision; for TSK-082, explain the published variance against actual revenue. Mark proposals as proposals. Do not add unsupported figures or claim that a forecast is an observed result. {#b:2ba4a98b}

### 3. Client-development and marketing plan {#b:5617c970}

Choose one practice area represented in the firm's book of business. Ask the tech tool to draft a short plan covering the clients you intend to reach, their likely needs, suitable communication channels, responsibilities, and a review date. Include a short website or directory blurb. Use the dashboard to explain your choice of practice area without disclosing client-identifying information. Do not invent market statistics, campaign results, or firm credentials. {#b:c4e9eb36}

Read Model Rules 7.1–7.3 and revise the plan and blurb yourself. Check every factual claim; remove unsupported comparisons, promises of outcomes, and false or misleading statements. Explain how your proposed communications meet the advertising and solicitation rules, including any restrictions relevant to contacting prospective clients. Submit the plan and blurb as drafts for partner approval; do not publish or contact anyone for this exercise. {#b:74766492}

### 4. Firm policy on client information {#b:741a23aa}

Draft a short policy for lawyers and nonlawyer staff using evolving technology with client information. Read Model Rules 1.1, 1.6, and 5.3 and use ABA Formal Opinion 512 (2024) on generative AI tools as a starting point. Explain what information may enter an approved tool, what must be withheld, how the firm evaluates a tool's confidentiality terms and limitations, and when consent or further review is needed. Do not treat removal of a client's name as sufficient protection if other details identify the client. {#b:484e9ba0}

Assign responsibility for approving tools, training staff, checking outputs, and responding to an accidental disclosure. Require a lawyer to verify facts, calculations, and authorities before relying on output or sharing it. Explain how the firm reviews its policy as technology changes. Your policy must preserve the lawyer's responsibility for competence, confidentiality, and supervision. {#b:9a0c625b}

### 5. Submit and revise {#b:7d87535c}

Submit your one-page memo and number-verification note, marketing plan and blurb, short firm policy, and record of sanitized prompts and revisions. Explain one output you corrected and why, and one task you retained for your own judgment. Use feedback to revise the deliverables and identify a supervision habit you will carry into your next matter. {#b:9415be9a}

## Assessment and feedback {#b:31bfd6bc}

Use the course's seven-heading instrument, with a separate 1–7 score under each heading: **Governing law; Strengths and weaknesses of both sides; Issues; Suggested solutions; Theory and themes; Elements to prevail; Liabilities and remedies**. Apply those headings to the firm's decisions: identify the governing professional rules, weigh the case for and against your proposals, identify management and confidentiality issues, recommend actions, explain a consistent plan, state what must be verified before acting, and address risks and corrective steps. Support each score with evidence from your deliverables. {#b:6dd5d0f5}

Lead feedback with what works and how to keep doing it, then give specific, manageable next steps. Focus on verified numbers, supportable public claims, protection of client information, and the decisions you made while supervising the tool. Keep feedback formative; never label work “good” or “bad.” Revise against the feedback and explain how your changes demonstrate progress toward your objectives. {#b:eb409222}
