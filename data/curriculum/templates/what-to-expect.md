## Purpose {#b:908d7ac2}

Welcome to the practicum. You will learn by handling matters, working with a partner, receiving feedback, and trying again. The course rewards steady, week-by-week work. You demonstrate progress through preparation, practice, and revision, rather than cramming at the end. {#b:8b4c128d}

Work cannot be postponed because each step prepares you for the next. Your interview supplies the facts for your memo; your memo informs your advice and negotiation plan. Your partner, clients, and opposing counsel also rely on you being ready. Feedback needs time to become a revision, and a revision needs time to become a habit. {#b:c2228d1f}

## The template {#b:a80ba01d}

Complete this short plan at the start of the course and revisit it each week. {#b:d732d481}

### 1. Your objectives {#b:893dc41c}

What do you want to learn about practicing law? Name two or three skills you want to demonstrate by the end of the course. {#b:cb034729}

### 2. Your weekly routine {#b:c3e085ec}

When will you prepare, meet with your partner, practice, and revise? Reserve time each week for these steps and for recording your work and time entries. {#b:bca47273}

### 3. Your next deadline {#b:3e17c493}

What is due next, what must happen first, and who depends on your work? Agree with your partner on responsibilities and check-in times. {#b:6a16b32d}

### 4. When you need help {#b:eabe1845}

If you fall behind or a task is unclear, contact your instructor and partner early. What question or obstacle will you raise, and what is your next manageable step? {#b:bbafc98a}

## Assessment and feedback {#b:40623eec}

Use feedback to identify what is working, keep practicing it, and choose a specific next step. Bring your weekly plan to check-ins so you can discuss how your work demonstrates progress toward your objectives. {#b:2c949d19}
